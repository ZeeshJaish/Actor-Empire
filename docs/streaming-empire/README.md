## Fixed v7 incorporation

Founding is presented through the ZIP's complete cinematic sequence: Wall,
application Case, ten-chapter Brand Board Wizard, signed incorporation deed,
and Activation. The visual chapters cover name, mark, type, colour, ident,
manifesto, storefront, opening map, optional executive seats, and the deed.
The ceremony remains a presentation over the fixed canonical transaction below;
it does not create a parallel wallet or incorporation economy.

The financial contract has no scale, loan, investor, infrastructure, or
executive choices:

| Movement | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Personal cash charged once | $85M |
| Legal, registration, foundational licensing, and platform setup consumed | $70M |
| Opening company treasury | $15M |

The founder starts with 100% ownership, remains CEO, raises $0 outside capital,
and creates $0 debt. Incorporation stores a `FIXED_V7` founding profile,
records the capital action and `FOUNDATION_CREATED` fact, clears the draft,
transitions `ELIGIBLE -> FOUNDING`, and queues the replayable
`FOUNDING_KEYNOTE`. Repeating the action cannot charge twice.

Infrastructure, extra founder capital, executives, rights, and expansion are
post-incorporation company decisions.

## Level 0 and earned reach

A new company enters a compact Level 0 headquarters with no purchased server
stack and zero technology levels. Level 0 is the incorporated foundation, not a
public operating footprint. Its demand range is the first regional load the
founder is planning to serve. There is no launch-region selector and no
joint-venture prerequisite.

Reach is recalculated from canonical capacity and technology:

| Level | Player-facing footprint | Required operating evidence | Modeled concurrent-demand range |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 0 | Foundation / no proven public footprint | Incorporated company | 130K–320K planned |
| 1 | Regional | 100K baseline streams, Delivery 6, Reliability 10 | 180K–520K |
| 2 | National | 1M baseline streams, Delivery 20, Reliability 18 | 700K–1.55M |
| 3 | Multi-region | 3M baseline streams, Delivery 30, Reliability 25 | 1.6M–3.8M |
| 4 | Global | 5M baseline streams, Delivery 35, Reliability 30, Content Operations 20, Product Experience 20 | 2.7M–6.4M |

All requirements on a row must pass. A capacity purchase alone does not grant
reach, subscribers, or market share. The same derived reach feeds HQ labels,
load-test demand, Original budget guidance, and future launch systems.

## Company control, leadership and governance

The founder can manually operate the company and is never forced to hire a
starter team. The Company Office opens a dedicated Leadership Suite with nine
specialist seats: COO, CFO, CTO, Chief Content Officer, Product Head, Marketing
Head, Advertising Head, International Head, and Security & Trust Head.

Every role has an external-search candidate and an internal-promotion route.
External candidates generally begin with higher mastery and compensation;
internal candidates cost less and begin with stronger loyalty and company
relationships. Appointments persist skill, loyalty, ambition, ethics, strategy,
founder and internal relationship, performance, level, experience, origin,
status, appointment week, and weekly compensation.

Four treasury-funded development programs advance only through canonical game
weeks and complete exactly once. Delegation persists rights-bid authority,
capacity headroom, weekly campaign authority, renewal margin, and incident
policy. A relevant executive enables each lane; missing leadership leaves the
founder in direct control.

Independent directors may be appointed while the founder owns 100%, but that
board remains advisory. Major motions affect the real delegation record.
Celebrity investors are optional, disclosed equity offers with capital,
dilution, participation, influence demands, and possible board nominees.
Only an accepted equity issuance can reduce founder ownership and make future
board votes binding. The service prevents a round from silently reducing
founder control below 51%.

Executive compensation, director fees, and accepted investor participation
enter the same weekly cash reconciliation as the rest of the platform.
Additional founder capital remains an explicit wallet-to-treasury transfer,
not revenue, debt, or dilution.

## Infrastructure and subscriptions

The founding map saves **Day-One Markets**, not a server purchase. A player may
open in one country, several countries, or several regions. The market scene
shows a stable game-world forecast for streaming viewers, growth, rights cost,
languages, launch workload, and the rivals already holding attention.

The later Build screen is the single authoritative place for infrastructure.
It reads the chosen markets and draws a suggested **Core Origin** blueprint,
but the player may move it, add more cities, change racks, or redesign the
network before spending company treasury. Every committed campus then stores
its shared-world city, rack count, and operating role.

- **Core Origin:** full catalog, encoding pipeline, and company control. One is
  always required and the save normalizer guarantees exactly one primary core.
- **Regional Hub:** carries most of the library nearer to a whole market.
- **Edge Cache:** cheaply holds popular titles close to viewers.

Players can place several campuses, grow any city from one to 96 racks, and
keep expanding it through rented cages, private halls, data-centre campuses,
and hyperscale campuses. Multiple halls and buildings are derived from rack
count; they are not arbitrary city caps. The production/Greenlight city
catalog supplies Build with location names, costs, infrastructure quality, and map
coordinates. Local facility, power, and operating economics therefore make
Los Angeles, Mumbai, Tokyo, Prague, and every other city materially different.

Starter, Essential, Growth, and Premiere remain migration-friendly presets,
not progression locks. Selecting one only draws an editable opening plan:

| Package | Baseline / burst streams | Base upfront cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Starter | 120K / 420K | $7.5M |
| Essential | 300K / 600K | $12M |
| Growth | 1.5M / 3M | $40M |
| Premiere | 5M / 10M | $110M |

Cloud-first, owned-network, and hybrid architecture modify cost, elasticity,
staffing, reliability, and build time. Safe, Standard, and Rushed rollout
modify cost, construction weeks, reliability, and technical debt.

The player-facing screen uses progressive disclosure. Its first answer is a
plain viewer forecast: startup speed, buffering risk, nearby-cache coverage,
and outage safety. `Show why` reveals regional latency, cache rate, and the
serving campus. Worldwide signal quality can be inspected while launch-night
demand still comes only from currently active markets, so a focused regional
opening remains viable without pretending a distant viewer has local service.

The exact configuration must be load-tested. The forecast uses low, likely,
and high demand from the reach the proposed stack would earn, never a founding
scale choice. This keeps the stored test valid after approval and can expose an
underpowered regional server choice before the company spends cash.
Basic, Premium, and Family prices are editable; planned ARPU is not recorded as
real ARPU before launch. Final approval is one company-treasury transaction,
and infrastructure revisions are idempotent.

After commitment, Network -> Capacity reads those same canonical placements;
it does not reconstruct cities from the legacy package label. Large campuses
render a representative hall plus the hidden rack count, avoiding dozens of
unnecessary mobile DOM nodes while retaining the full saved capacity.

## Rights, Originals, and the launch slate

The content chain preserves canonical project ownership:

1. Released titles from player-controlled production houses can be linked by
   project ID. This creates no internal sale or consolidated profit.
2. One real external title can be licensed by negotiating territory, duration,
   exclusivity, minimum guarantee, and the platform/licensor revenue split.
   Treasury pays the guarantee once; the contract has an absolute expiry week.
3. Audience-gap analysis seeds the first Original mandate. The owned platform
   remains commissioner and rights holder; a player-controlled production
   house physically produces it through the existing Greenlight flow. A
   commission requires at least $5M of real company treasury.
4. The commission reserves treasury, creates a real script and production
   handoff, returns unused funding after Greenlight, and queues the fact-backed
   first-Original announcement.
5. The launch slate programs canonical titles across twelve game weeks with
   movie/series release patterns and marketing plans. It blocks a slate with no
   Original or fewer than three titles and warns about genre concentration,
   long gaps, missing series, quiet Original marketing, and incomplete
   delivery. Entries are re-resolved against canonical projects and current
   license windows before locking, so fabricated IDs, forged sources, duplicate
   titles, and expired rights cannot enter the company record.

## Launch Command and Premiere Night

Phase 8 is the first complete platform-launch decision. Home exposes Launch
Command early as a truthful diagnostic; its commit gate remains closed until
the required canonical records exist. Its readiness forecast is derived again
whenever it opens:

- incorporation and launchable company lifecycle
- committed infrastructure and its real construction-ready week
- exact load-test result and burst capacity
- at least three canonical slate titles
- final delivery of the Original through the existing production workflow
- license coverage in each title's programmed release week
- company-treasury runway
- high-case premiere demand derived from earned reach, slate depth, series
  retention, and Original marketing

Company, Content, and Tech blockers deep-link back to their operating rooms.
Runway and capacity pressure are warnings rather than arbitrary hard locks. At
the final gate the founder chooses one of three launch-night responses:

| Response | Tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Open the gates | No new cost; trusts the commissioned burst capacity |
| Reserve cloud burst | Temporary peak protection paid once by company treasury |
| Stagger the premiere | Flattens demand for a smaller treasury cost and 8% fewer opening conversions |

`commitOwnedStreamingLaunch` creates one immutable `launchCommit`, charges the
chosen response once, commits opening subscribers and operational health,
records `LAUNCH_COMMITTED`, changes `FOUNDING -> ACTIVE`, and only then queues
`LAUNCH_NIGHT` using the ledger fact ID. Repeating the action cannot charge
cash, reroll subscribers, or enqueue another outcome. Skipping or replaying the
cinematic changes only its viewed status. Detailed audience reactions and
weekly movement remain Phase 9+.

## Complete weekly CEO loop

Phase 10 connects the owned platform to `processGameWeek`. The processor runs
after the real Actor Empire calendar advances, including year rollover, and
uses the resulting absolute game week. It exits without mutation unless the
platform is live, has a committed launch, is at least one week beyond launch,
and has not already recorded `owned-streaming-week:[absoluteWeek]`.

The session is a four-part operating ritual:

1. Read one urgent issue, one opportunity, one upcoming event and the essential
   subscriber, churn, runway and delivery signals.
2. Optionally lock one weekly mandate: audience push, reliability guard, or
   retention spotlight. Every option previews cost, benefit and risk.
3. Return to the normal game and advance the real Actor Empire week. Leaving
   without a mandate uses visible balanced autopilot; it does not punish the
   player.
4. Re-enter Platform HQ to receive the committed result, subscriber
   join/reactivation/cancellation waterfall, platform cash contribution,
   causal drivers and the next-week hook.

Weekly outcomes use `createDeterministicRng` with the platform seed and absolute
week. Acquisition, churn, engagement and demand respond to slate freshness,
genre breadth, Original presence, price pressure, the chosen mandate and a
small seeded variance. Peak concurrency is tested against commissioned burst
capacity; playback responds to utilization, reliability, technical debt and
the chosen mandate.

Cash reconciliation is explicit:

```text
subscription cash
- external licensor participation
- infrastructure operating cost
- active executive compensation
- financing interest
- weekly mandate cost
= net treasury movement
```

Original funding and license guarantees were paid in earlier phases. Their
weekly content amortization affects accounting contribution but is not charged
to treasury again. Each result stores a compact operations block inside the
weekly snapshot, commits `WEEK_CHECKPOINT` and `METRICS_COMMITTED` ledger facts,
closes the saved mandate, and caps both report and decision history for mobile
saves. Reprocessing the same week cannot duplicate cash, subscribers,
decisions or history.

Phase 10 originally deferred competitors, market share, per-title viewing,
quarter reviews and board events. Phases 11–20 now layer those systems onto
the same exactly-once weekly record.

## Platform Wars, global expansion and awards

Phase 20 gives Market Room a persistent competitive world. Five fictional CEO
characters operate the existing rival platform records with visible strategy,
cash, subscribers, technology, catalog strength, prestige, aggression,
cooldowns, mistakes, preferences and player memory. An eligible move spends
rival cash, writes its before/after balance, enters cooldown, and can misfire.
The player can answer market pressure or an executive-poaching offer from the
company treasury; poaching targets a real active appointment.

Home Market plus six expansion territories appear on the Global Delivery Map.
Reach and technology qualify a route without forcing a joint venture. The
chosen regional approach determines capital, game-week build time, permanent
weekly cost, acquisition opportunity, localization depth and peak server
load. Only one localization build can run at a time.

Each processed live week commits one modeled market-share snapshot from
canonical subscriber records. Platform Wars, Market HQ, Analytics, quarterly
rival reporting and the annual jury consume this same record. Every 52 live
weeks, five Streaming Awards categories resolve from subscriber, engagement,
Original, playback, technology, regional and share evidence. Awards have no
purchase or paid-influence path.

## Visual asset pipeline

`StreamingVisualScene` is the shared scene shell for registration, Level 0 HQ,
command deck, content studio, network operations centre, market room, company
office, and viewer lab. Every registry entry includes a focal point, palette,
accessible description, and authored CSS fallback, so missing art never leaves
a broken screen.

Streaming workspaces share an accessible modal scope with initial focus,
keyboard containment, Escape handling, stacked-dialog safety, and opener focus
restoration. Viewer Mode progressively renders catalog rows in twelve-title
pages, deduplicates opening/category shelves, and lazy-loads poster media.

Source art belongs in `assets/streaming/source`. Run:

```bash
npm run assets:streaming
```

The pipeline uses `cwebp`, outputs 1400×788 WebP at quality 84, archives the
processed files in `assets/streaming/scenes`, and copies runtime assets to
`public/assets/streaming/scenes`. Runtime filenames must match
`services/streamingVisualAssets.ts`; `assets/streaming/streaming-art-manifest.json`
records the shared pipeline settings and art direction.
