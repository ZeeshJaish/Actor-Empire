## Failures found and resolved

1. **Undelivered streams earned money.** The C6 red test reproduced ad/pass and store receipts at zero commissioned capacity. The canonical viewing allocation now derives a delivery fraction from commissioned burst concurrency, the existing 9% peak-concurrency demand conversion, and active incident capacity loss. It also applies the Build network's per-country covered share before paid/free/shared viewing or rent/buy receipts are created. The same fixture now produces **$0** at zero capacity. A 20,000-stream network lowers ad/pass gross from **$34,237** to **$25,830**; an active fibre-cut incident lowers it to **$21,550**. A 2,000-stream store network lowers title gross from **$10,142** to **$4,646**. A US-only network with US and UK launch markets produces no UK viewing, ads, passes, rental, or purchase receipts; reachable US receipts remain positive and reconcile. Legacy active saves with no commissioned facilities retain their placeholder-capacity behavior. The zero-capacity replay is deterministic.
2. **Dark or partially covered countries could overbill subscribers.** A red C6 assertion showed $316,222.80 monthly UK subscription revenue with zero UK network coverage. The player offer registry now removes physically unreachable countries when a commissioned facility map exists. Partial-country reach also limits the potential paying households and the initial player-subscriber seed, while the subscription price for each reached household stays intact. Paid viewing is not multiplied by country reach a second time. A second red test showed an existing UK cohort still billing $142,087.70 after its only route disappeared; the following weekly customer transition now ends that player cohort and charges $0. AI customer behavior is unchanged.
3. **Old saves were refused by the integrity verifier.** A newer manifest protected an expanded world-economy projection including audience choice; previously signed generations used the earlier seven-state projection. The verifier now recomputes that exact earlier projection from the payload and accepts its digest without accepting changed protected money. The save-integrity and generation audits pass. In the local game browser, all three displayed slots—JJJ, morkv, and Tony Stark—opened to their career home screens without deleting them.
4. **The 400-year save-size audit was misclassified.** The roughly 6 MB payload is primarily the *current* 197-country audience/customer state, not unbounded history. More importantly, the live save path uses IndexedDB for the full career and intentionally degrades its 600 KB localStorage mirror to slot metadata for large saves. The old audit's 3.5 MB full-save assertion did not match this contract. It now checks metadata-only mirror selection and full JSON reload preserving 197 countries and rights. This corrects the audit, not the payload size; IndexedDB availability and real-device quota behavior remain worth watching.
5. **A perpetual right looked as if it were expiring.** The live command card rendered `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` as an enormous remaining-week count, while the next-week brief called that same title the next expiry risk. A failing C6 first-week assertion reproduced the brief error. Perpetual licences now have an explicit card label and are excluded from the expiry hook; finite active rights remain eligible.

The C6 failures were exposed by this phase; the save-open failure followed a later manifest-shape change. A `processGameWeek` regression advances a launch-night platform through its actual first live week, then checks both compaction/migration and signed generation save/reload without changing receipts. A separate isolated browser career now exercises the actual UI from an already-commissioned network through opening night and its first live week. The pricing phone check used an isolated fixture, not a player's real save.

## Manual Build-to-live follow-up on 2026-09-24

On a new save-isolated origin (`127.0.0.1:49331`), the `C6 Phone QA` career started with one filed, cleared US market but **no infrastructure setup, draft, pending order, or capacity**. From the actual 393×852 Build UI, three Titan racks and twenty Meridian cloud units were added. The likely-night rehearsal held: one market playing, none buffering or down, with a playable viewer preview. The in-game commission agreement said **$26.5M due at execution** from a $200M treasury. After the player clicked Commission, treasury showed **$161.4M**—roughly **$38.6M** deducted—and the programme said **$39M released**. This is a quote/charge mismatch, not a rounding difference. The programme also described the three physical racks plus twenty cloud units as **23 racks**.

Six normal age-up weeks moved the programme to Ready for Opening Night. The 11/11 go/no-go board opened the signal; the launch report showed a 1.76M concurrent peak against 2.05M protected capacity, 0% failures, and a clean US stream. The next normal week produced a detailed operating result: 825,870 paid accounts, $1.4M subscription cash, $14K ads, $7K transactions, $8.3M cash operating costs, and about $6.8M net treasury use. Ad and transaction receipts are expected here because this isolated career deliberately enables a hybrid offer. Reloading the game, selecting the signed slot, and reopening it retained week 19 and the live career. This run used a disposable QA save only; it did not mutate any existing player save.

Red/green C6 tests traced the money mismatch to the old infrastructure forecast charging a different amount than the canonical Build network quote. For explicit regional Build plans, the commission and weekly operating forecast now use the same quote the Build UI displays; legacy non-regional drafts keep their prior formula. The same tests found that the old network sanitizer did not recognize an Ashburn (`ASH`) server site, and that normalizing a twenty-unit cloud allocation reduced it to sixteen units because a virtual allocation was capped by a physical site footprint. The sanitizer now accepts the server-site catalogue; cloud leases preserve up to 384 purchased compute units, while physical rooms retain their building ceiling. The opening programme now counts only physical racks and describes the wider network as cities being brought online. Focused C6, opening-programme, regional-network lifecycle, and C6 TypeScript checks pass after these changes.
