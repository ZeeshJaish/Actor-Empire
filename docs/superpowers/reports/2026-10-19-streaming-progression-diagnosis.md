# Streaming progression diagnosis — 19 October 2026

## Verdict

The desired sequence—small market, modest catalogue, reinvestment in content/features, justified price changes, paid expansion, then sustained rivalry—is only partially connected. Current evidence does not justify calling that complete gameplay loop ready. The game does not simply award profit for every decision: all four small-market control cases lost money. The more material findings are excessively blunt country-independent title quotes, a live pricing mutation gate, and a reproduced Product Lab retention/acquisition integration gap.

No production balance changes or live-career edits were made for this diagnosis. All new scenario/probe code was disposable. Existing dirty repairs and economic changes were preserved.

## What the preceding payback figures measured

The five preceding full-cost runs were **US only**, not worldwide. The network had nine racks across three US cities; title licences explicitly covered US. They were prepared launch cases with $3B test equity, not a normal small founder career or profit-funded regional expansion. A one-country US launch is itself a large market in the simulation. Those results cannot certify the small-market progression requested here.

Market entry is country-based. Physical network regions and city coverage are separate. New Zealand and Australia below are two countries in the same Oceania delivery region, not two world regions.

## New 26-week controlled cases

Four independent prepared subscription launches, each with $300M test equity, a common paid $5M Original, quoted non-exclusive second-window movie licences, actual market-entry quotes, $85M incorporation/setup and quoted network capital/recurring service costs. No cost, title quality or household budget was boosted during the run. These are comparisons between launch setups, not a single career that paid for expansion out of profits.

Running cash contribution deducts current operating costs and any additional maintenance expense; it precedes noncash content amortization and is not full investment payback. Finance includes content recognition. Each case had the Original in addition to its licensed movies. A 26-week loss does not prove a country can never become viable; it does show these launch configurations have not established the intended self-funded growth path.

Licence cash was $22.5M for the small slate, $92.25M for the improved NZ slate and $157.5M for the two-country slate. Network capital was $3.75M for one Auckland rack, $7.5M for Auckland plus Sydney. Entry/compliance was $2.4M for NZ and $13.2M combined for NZ+AU. Changing price from $3.99 to $6.99 reduced accounts enough that receipts also fell; audience did not stay fixed when price rose. Better content increased audience substantially, but added investment and amortization as well.

All 104 actual processGameWeek calls completed. The checks confirm paid-stock reconciliation, content recognition bounded by its cost basis, and no subscription billing in an unopened country. Country records for the two-market case show NZ and AU receipts only. These are not new end-to-end browser or normal-founder-career tests.

## Reproduced gaps

### Live repricing is blocked

Every launched test career called the real `saveStreamingPricingPlan` with a revised plan price. It returned `changed:false, reason:INVALID_STATE`. The gate is `platform.launchCommit` at `services/streamingLaunchProgram.ts:538`. Current launch pricing forecasts can compare offers, but that is not a supported post-launch save transaction. The requested “improve the service, then charge a justified higher price” loop needs live plan/revision/billing semantics.

### Paid Kids benefits do not reach canonical customer choice/retention

A separate prepared-prerequisite integration uses the existing product audit's research/technology prerequisites and the actual `startStreamingProductDevelopment` and due-completion services. This is not proof of the entire normal research career. It pays $18M capital once, rejects duplicate purchase and activates Kids after its actual development interval.

The launched product reports acquisition +0.001 and churn −0.0015, but the canonical customer offer is byte-identical to the control. Its country experience/churn input also remains identical. With matched RNG and an actual next game week:

| Branch | Paid accounts | Subscription receipts | Product weekly cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Control |11,413|$9,891|$0|
| Kids active |11,413|$9,891|$250,000|

This is a real integration defect for the tested benefits. Product growth/churn deltas are applied to legacy acquisition/churn arithmetic, then `worldCustomerOutcome` replaces actual joins/cancellations at `services/streamingWeeklyLoop.ts:718`. The canonical offer's retention input considers catalogue quality/freshness and delivery, but omits these product benefits. Some other feature and technology mechanisms already have separate connections; this finding must not be generalized to every feature producing zero benefit.

### Regional economics are too blunt for the requested progression

The current title quote accepts DOMESTIC/MULTI_REGION/GLOBAL, but not the actual country IDs or country market economics. The same domestic title quote therefore does not distinguish a small NZ audience from US. The test still paid every current quote. At small scale, the $55K country-service minimum plus standard $100K enforcement base already exceeds receipts by a large margin, before other infrastructure/content costs. Those costs need an explicit service/staffing scope if a genuinely smaller operation is intended; simply reducing bills to force a positive result would not be a defensible repair.

## What competition and expansion already support

- The market service supports paid EXPANSION clearance after launch. Opening and expansion cannot sell the same country twice. Rights, service coverage and active-country checks bound customers and playback. The living-markets and coverage audits pass.
- Canonical audience allocation evaluates competing prices, catalogue strength, feature appeal, localization, trust and finite household budgets. A controlled mature-offer comparison changes only rival offers, retaining the same cohorts and total monthly budget of $54,581,391. Player target subscriptions fall from 101,571 to 87,163 (about 14.2%); rival subscriptions rise. These are demand-allocation targets, not observed switched accounts in a multi-year career.
- AI platform-war commitment tests pass for real cash obligations and replay safety. Neither these tests nor the controlled choice comparison certify a complete adaptive late-game career.

## Recommended next implementation

1. Connect actual installed, enabled and entitled product features to one canonical country/cohort choice and retention model. Family benefits require family content and family users; other audiences need not pay more. Keep installation/development/staffing/weekly costs, prevent double applying both legacy and canonical boosts, and never create a flat revenue-per-subscriber reward without funded receipts.
2. Add a real live pricing revision flow with a disclosed effective date, appropriate billing/annual-term handling, churn/price sensitivity and preview of risk. Preserve signed content agreements and do not rewrite past revenue.
3. Make new licence quotes and local service tiers reflect the actual contracted countries, monetization rights, duration, staffing/SLA and seller competition. Small scope can cost less because fewer rights/services are supplied. Buying narrow cheap rights and later enabling extra territories/modes must require those additional rights and payment. Do not peg seller price to a manipulable buyer plan price or current treasury. Existing signed fees stay intact.
4. Verify an actual one-career path from modest paid launch to real reinvestment, rights/localization/network-qualified expansion and later catalogue renewal. Content and feature investment may improve retention/willingness; raising price or entering another country may still reduce cash and profit.
5. Test later-game saturation, priced renewals, rival improvements and shocks over multiple seeds. Healthy mature operations may keep earning; neglected/overpriced services must be able to lose share. No compulsory failure, automatic profit, inflated advertiser pool or increased household wallets.

A small local operation and worldwide operation need different feasible scales, not arbitrary difficulty multipliers. Full payback should follow the actual capital and recurring commitments, not a fixed promised week. The next green light needs this linked progression test, not only separate feature-unit tests or a prepared profitable large-market case.
