# Stats-Driven Industry Simulation Remap Design

**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** Approved roadmap architecture; detailed B2 specification remains the next review gate  
**Scope:** Rival production studios, rival streaming platforms, player-interaction escalation, and Project B remapping

## Decision

Actor Empire will not simulate every AI-only company by making it execute the complete player workflow invisibly. Rival production studios and rival streaming platforms will use a shared deterministic, stats-driven, scheduled-event simulation.

The complete existing systems remain authoritative whenever an outcome becomes player-controlled, player-interactive, legally binding, financially exact, talent-binding, or publicly permanent.

The central rule is:

> Statistical simulation decides why and how strongly an AI company wants to act. Existing canonical systems execute the action whenever the player, money, rights, talent, ownership, or a public project is involved.

This simplifies private causes without simplifying visible consequences.

## Why the roadmap changes

The completed core Platform AI currently gives five flagship platforms a detailed weekly lifecycle covering sourcing, rights, commissions, production, localization, research, markets, scheduling, release, learning, finance, and distress. The wider regional and generated streaming ecosystem already proves that a smaller capability-and-event model can create credible company movement.

The original Project B plan would have extended the flagship model to every production studio. That would cause many companies to search material, develop scripts, evaluate finance, package talent, and manage production in the background even when the player never observes those decisions.

That approach has three avoidable costs:

- repeated consumption of finite title, synopsis, and script-template material;
- growing project, decision, job, and history records in long-generation saves; and
- increasing Process Week work on mobile devices.

The remap preserves causal depth at every player-visible boundary while replacing invisible procedural work with a compact model that can support more companies and longer careers.

## Approaches considered

### Full invisible-player simulation

Every rival executes the detailed player workflow. It offers rich internal lineage but stores and processes large amounts of information the player cannot inspect. This is rejected as the default AI-only model.

### Pure random project generation

Companies periodically create releases from a genre and budget roll. It is fast but cannot explain strategy, finance, relationships, specialization, or long-term learning. This is rejected because it would feel arbitrary.

### Stats-driven scheduled simulation with materialization

Companies carry strong identity, capability, condition, and bounded memory. They evaluate only due decisions, create compact project fingerprints, and materialize exact records at meaningful boundaries. This is the approved approach.

## Simulation layers

### Company identity

Slow-changing traits describe what a company is:

- scale and launch class;
- company strategy and risk tolerance;
- preferred genres, formats, audiences, languages, and markets;
- prestige versus commercial intent;
- franchise appetite;
- normal budget range; and
- desired release volume.

### Capability

Production studios use development, creative, production, finance, marketing, distribution, negotiation, and talent-relationship competence.

Streaming platforms use content judgement, technology, catalogue management, localization, finance, discovery/marketing, negotiation, and market-operations competence.

Capabilities are normalized, versioned balance inputs. They are not exposed as raw rival-company status labels.

### Current condition

Faster-changing state includes cash, debt, runway, free capacity, momentum, creative form, genre fatigue, recent hit strength, audience trust, catalogue need, financial pressure, and bilateral relationships.

### Bounded memory

Companies remember only decision-relevant summaries: recent genre and budget results, commercial/prestige form, partnerships, project fingerprints, market performance, franchise fatigue, cancellations, and turnaround outcomes. Memory uses bounded rolling aggregates rather than an unbounded narrative log.

## Decision lifecycle

Each company stores stable next-due weeks for content, production review, release review, finance, expansion, and capability growth. A weekly coordinator performs light recurring settlement, then invokes only decisions due in the entered week.

A content decision follows:

1. **Opportunity:** determine whether cash, runway, capacity, cadence, or a commission creates room for a project.
2. **Need:** construct a compact content fingerprint for the missing genre, format, audience, language, budget, strategic intent, and distribution purpose.
3. **Feasibility:** check money, capacity, rights, repetition, congestion, and producer availability at an aggregate level.
4. **Forecast:** estimate creative, execution, commercial, prestige, and downside potential with competence-shaped error.
5. **Commitment:** persist only the selected material decision and its exact economic or rights boundary.
6. **Outcome:** resolve saved milestones and update company condition and bounded learning.

Forecast skill improves decision accuracy but never creates a quality or commercial floor. All material uncertainty is deterministically seeded and persisted before it can affect money, rights, the player, or public history.

## Content fingerprints and variety

A private AI concept begins as structured data rather than a complete script:

- movie or series;
- primary and secondary genre;
- subgenre;
- tone and theme;
- setting and period;
- target audience;
- original language;
- budget band;
- commercial/prestige balance;
- franchise relationship;
- creative-risk level;
- star-power target; and
- intended release path.

The engine compares fingerprints against the company's recent slate and the recent industry. Reused title structures, genre/theme combinations, settings, audiences, sequel patterns, and near-duplicate fingerprints receive cooldowns or novelty penalties.

Titles, synopses, cast presentation, and other expensive narrative material are generated only when a project crosses the public or player-interaction boundary. They are then saved permanently and never rerolled.

## Outcome model

The model does not use one flat average.

- Creative potential combines concept, development, creative judgement, genre fit, originality, and patience.
- Execution potential combines production competence, budget suitability, capacity pressure, talent package, schedule, and saved variance.
- Commercial potential combines audience fit, demand, star power, marketing, distribution, brand, timing, and competition.
- Prestige potential combines material, creative package, studio identity, campaign suitability, and execution.
- Financial risk combines cost, cash, runway, debt, scale, strategy, and downside exposure.

Every fingerprint has a suitable budget range. Underspending harms execution; suitable spending is efficient; overspending has diminishing creative returns and higher downside. Wealth increases options without guaranteeing hits.

Success changes company capability slowly. Momentum can change faster but cools toward normal. Scale increases opportunity and operating burden. Repetition, overextension, and franchise fatigue prevent runaway winners.

## Materialization boundary

AI-only private work stays compact. A complete canonical record is required when:

- a project is publicly announced;
- named talent is attached or booked;
- the player is hired;
- a player-controlled studio receives a commission;
- a player-controlled platform or studio buys, sells, or licenses rights;
- money crosses company boundaries;
- production becomes a public commitment;
- a title receives a release date;
- a release enters IMDb, box office, streaming results, awards, or permanent history; or
- ownership changes while work remains active.

At materialization, the stable seed and saved milestone state expand into the existing canonical project, production, talent, rights, finance, release, and presentation systems. Materialization may add detail but cannot change previously resolved facts.

## Bidding and rights boundary

Project A remains unchanged as the contract and settlement authority. The stats-driven model supplies bidder eligibility and appetite inputs; it does not replace the bidding room.

The following remain authoritative:

- exact eligible countries, territory, window, duration, and exclusivity;
- bidder financial eligibility and restrictions;
- the 15-second room clock, equal six-second response cooldown, extensions, and hard cap;
- platform offer revisions, reductions, restructuring, final offers, and withdrawals;
- guarantee, backend, recoupment, cap, production funding, localization, and renewal terms;
- same-session restoration and later-market refresh; and
- exact offer-to-contract and backend settlement.

The shared valuation adapter derives expected title value, fixed-exposure ceiling, preferred deal shape, genre/audience interest, strategic-country demand, catalogue gap, subscriber opportunity, risk, negotiation, relationship, and localization capability from the platform's saved stats. Fixed commitments respect affordability; uncapped backend may exceed the signing forecast when attributable revenue genuinely outperforms.

Impossible rights never enter a room. A platform may still strategically pay more for a broad remaining-territory lot because one valuable market or competitive exclusion makes the package important.

## Commission boundary

Platform content need may create an individual commission brief. Future-output and automatic multi-picture deals remain deferred.

- AI platform to AI studio: use compact development and production milestones until a public or contractual boundary requires detail.
- AI platform to player studio: use the existing SMS offer, Production House brief, script selection, Greenlight, budget cap, producer fee, production, delivery, and relationship flow.
- Player platform commissions: continue through the detailed owned-platform workflow.

Production funding is restricted project money, producer fees are income, and production ownership does not silently transfer.

## Player ownership and delegation

The rival shortcut applies only while a company is AI-controlled.

On acquisition, cash, debt, markets, capabilities, rights, catalogue, public projects, commitments, relationships, progress, and mistakes remain. Compact active projects materialize at their last saved milestone without restart or reroll. AI-only cost, speed, rescue, and decision advantages stop.

A player-controlled subsidiary may operate through its existing Command Centre mandates. Delegation can use the shared decision model, but all actions execute through player-standard money, time, capacity, rights, production, and approval rules. Protected decisions remain subject to player approval.

## Presentation

Player-facing depth comes from causal continuity rather than raw internal dashboards:

`company identity → strategic need → project decision → public commitment → production result → release result → company consequence → future strategy`

Material events project into existing News, Forbes, IMDb, box office, awards, X, and Instagram surfaces. Every projection references the same canonical company, project, contract, and event IDs. Importance thresholds, channel cadence, cooldowns, and bounded histories prevent spam.

Project B may ship the canonical event hooks and restrained reactions required to understand the simulation. The larger Shared Industry Reactions and Media World remains a later project built on those saved facts.

## Persistence and performance contract

AI saves retain company identity, current stats, finance summary, capacity, due-week checkpoints, bounded learning, compact active projects, public commitments, canonical transactions, and permanent releases.

They do not retain rejected private concepts, every marketplace item inspected, unused narrative drafts, invisible meetings, or redundant decision explanations.

The weekly cost should scale primarily with due companies and active material projects rather than total historical projects. Indices replace repeated full-world scans. Histories remain bounded. Existing A8P Process Week and A8S save-integrity protections remain regression boundaries.

## Remapped Project B sequence

### B1 — Canonical Studio State, Control, and Migration — complete

Retain the existing studio identity, competence, finance, capacity, controller, migration, and save foundation.

### B2 — Shared Statistical Intelligence Kernel

Build the common company-stat model, scheduled decisions, forecast uncertainty, learning, fatigue, explanations, controller boundary, deterministic checkpoints, and shadow/parity instrumentation.

### B3 — Content Fingerprint and Variety Engine

Build compact content intentions, novelty control, lazy narrative materialization, and deterministic identity for originals, licensed works, franchises, and individual commissions.

### B4 — Streaming Platform AI Migration

Move the five flagship platforms and wider operator ecosystem onto the shared private-decision model while preserving bidding, commissions, rights, markets, economy, distress, acquisition, and player handoff.

### B5 — Production Studio Slate and Greenlight Simulation

Turn B1 studio stats into bounded automatic slates, development commitments, budget decisions, holds, abandonment, and individual platform-commission fulfilment without invisible player-screen simulation.

### B6 — AI Production, Release, and Commercial Results

Resolve compact AI-only production milestones, public talent packages, release strategy, box office or streaming outcomes, rights commerce, franchise learning, and awards. Player-linked projects use the full existing workflow.

### B7 — Player Interaction, Ownership, and World Presentation

Complete materialization, acquisition handoff, player-standard subsidiary delegation, legacy-generator retirement, and consistent News/Forbes/IMDb/social projections.

### B8 — Balance, Mobile Performance, and Long-Run Verification

Certify migration, deterministic replay, 10/25/50/100/400-year outcomes, repetition limits, save growth, mobile Process Week budgets, bidding and commission parity, acquisition handoff, company churn, and final reports.

## Migration strategy

The replacement is incremental:

1. Add the shared B2 kernel without changing canonical gameplay outcomes.
2. Add the B3 fingerprint engine alongside current generators.
3. Migrate AI-only streaming decisions behind parity tests in B4.
4. Migrate studio slate and release generation in B5–B6.
5. Complete player handoff and presentation before removing legacy paths in B7.
6. Remove a legacy path only after its replacement passes focused, save/reload, and adjacent regression coverage.

Old saves normalize forward without deleting companies, projects, contracts, rights, payments, talent bookings, releases, or family/career history.

## Deferred scope

- future-output and automatic multi-picture agreements;
- actor firing, breach, replacement litigation, and the full legal pack;
- a separate full social-media melodrama system before canonical simulation events stabilize; and
- forcing AI-only companies through player UI state.

## Acceptance criteria

The remap is complete only when:

- AI-only studios and platforms use scheduled stats-driven private decisions;
- player interaction and canonical transactions retain full system depth;
- Project A remains the only streaming-rights and settlement authority;
- bidding and commission behaviour retains or improves its existing strategic depth;
- content variety remains inside approved long-run repetition limits;
- company strength matters without guaranteeing success;
- acquisition and delegation preserve facts and use player-standard rules;
- no legacy save loses canonical history;
- Process Week and save growth remain within approved mobile budgets; and
- the final 400-year verification completes in practical engineering time.
