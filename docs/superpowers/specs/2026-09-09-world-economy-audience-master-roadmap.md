# Actor Empire World Economy and Audience Master Roadmap

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Audience:** Actor Empire development and future implementation chats
**Status:** WE1, WE2, and WE3 delivered in shadow mode; focused validation and production build green
**Current next phase:** WE4 — Streaming Plan Choice and Market Competition

## Purpose

Build one authoritative, country-based population and audience foundation for Actor Empire. The same people and households must eventually power streaming subscriptions, viewing, advertising, rentals, piracy, cinema footfalls, box office, fame, consumer products, and other audience-facing systems.

This roadmap does not simulate billions of individual records. It uses deterministic aggregate cohorts so that the world feels populated and economically constrained without making weekly progression, mobile performance, or long-running saves impractical.

## Core rule

> Population is the source of demand. Streaming subscribers, shared viewers, pirates, cinema visitors, customers, fans, and non-participants are different outcomes drawn from that population—not separate people generated by each feature.

No industry may assume that every living person participates. A person or household may use no streaming service, visit no cinema, use one or several services, share an account, pirate content, visit cinemas occasionally, or participate in several entertainment activities at once.

## Architectural direction

The world economy has three layers:

1. **Canonical population:** countries, households, demographics, income, access, languages, culture, and economic conditions.
2. **Audience cohorts:** aggregated groups with entertainment budgets, preferences, habits, access, and participation tendencies.
3. **Industry adapters:** streaming, cinema, advertising, merchandise, and future systems translate those cohorts into industry-specific decisions.

Streaming and cinema therefore share people and economic constraints but do not share one participation flag. Streaming adoption and cinema attendance are separate decisions.

## Existing systems to reuse

Implementation must extend or consume the current canonical systems instead of creating parallel replacements:

- `services/streamingAudienceMarket.ts` for the existing global population, adoption, household, overlap, trend, and persona concepts;
- `services/streamingDayOneMarkets.ts` for authored country markets, languages, growth, competition, market-entry conditions, and localization expectations;
- `services/streamingWeeklyLoop.ts` for player-platform weekly acquisition, churn, reactivation, revenue, costs, and history;
- `services/streamingEconomyCore.ts` for currency-safe revenue, cost, runway, and valuation calculations;
- the streaming pricing and product configuration for exact plans, discounts, advertising, rentals, purchases, premium access, and other commercial choices;
- the existing Platform AI and shared statistical intelligence systems for lightweight AI-platform decisions;
- canonical catalogue, rights, localization, release, marketing, crisis, media, and research systems as demand inputs;
- the existing weekly progression, deterministic random, save migration, history compression, and recovery systems.

The current audience market is primarily a derived presentation model. During this programme, its useful definitions will be moved behind or projected from the canonical world engine so that dashboards report the same population that drives gameplay.

## Non-goals

- Do not create one saved character for every person in the world.
- Do not reproduce live real-world statistics or laws dynamically.
- Do not force the player to manage cohorts individually.
- Do not make the cheapest platform automatically win.
- Do not make streaming membership or cinema attendance universal.
- Do not build the full cinema-chain game before its own approved project.
- Do not run AI platforms through player UI workflows.

## Shared population invariants

- Country cohort totals must never exceed the eligible country population.
- Household, viewer, account, subscription, ticket, and footfall counts are distinct quantities.
- One household may support multiple viewers and subscriptions.
- A shared viewer is not automatically a paying subscriber.
- A pirated view is not direct platform revenue.
- Cinema visits count attendance events; unique moviegoers are counted separately.
- A cohort may participate in several industries, one industry, or none.
- All saved decisions use stable IDs, absolute game weeks, deterministic calculations, and versioned migration.
- AI advantages stop when the player takes control if the relevant system marks them as AI-only.
- Historical summaries may be compressed, but material population, economic, company, release, and dynasty outcomes must remain reconstructable.

# Phase WE1 — Canonical Country Population

## Purpose

Create the authoritative country and regional population registry from which every later cohort and industry calculation begins.

## Core work

- establish stable country and region IDs;
- store or deterministically derive population, households, household size, age bands, income bands, urbanization, languages, connectivity, device access, payment access, and cinema access;
- add macroeconomic state such as income growth, inflation pressure, unemployment pressure, and consumer confidence;
- progress population and economic conditions by absolute game week;
- provide regional fallback profiles for countries without hand-authored data;
- migrate the current streaming market population and growth values into the canonical model;
- expose read-only selectors for future streaming, cinema, advertising, and other adapters;
- seed old saves deterministically from their current game year without changing the player's existing finances or subscribers.

## Completion gate

- every supported country resolves to one canonical population profile;
- global, regional, and country totals reconcile;
- progression and reload produce identical results;
- no streaming or future cinema consumer creates an independent population total;
- old saves migrate safely;
- long-run population values remain valid and bounded.

# Phase WE2 — Population Cohorts and Household Budgets

**Delivery status:** Complete in shadow mode. Canonical country cohorts, non-participant households, finite entertainment budgets, save migration, weekly progression, and read-only Audience Market projections are implemented. Live subscriber and revenue allocation remains owned by the existing streaming economy until WE4.

## Purpose

Divide each country's people and households into lightweight behavioural cohorts with finite entertainment budgets.

## Core work

- define sparse country cohorts using income, household/life stage, entertainment appetite, access, price sensitivity, content taste, loyalty, and legal/sharing tendencies;
- reuse and deepen the existing family, value, fandom, prestige, local-first, and habitual-streamer personas;
- calculate disposable entertainment budgets without assigning spending to an industry yet;
- support people outside the commercial entertainment market;
- allow cohort composition and budgets to evolve across eras and economic events;
- avoid a full Cartesian explosion by materializing only meaningful cohort combinations.

## Completion gate

- every country population reconciles across cohort and non-participant pools;
- budgets are finite, country-appropriate, and responsive to economic conditions;
- cohort generation is deterministic and mobile-safe;
- no individual-person save records are required.

# Phase WE3 — Participation and Access Model

**Delivery status:** Complete in shadow mode. Canonical streaming/cinema eligibility, interest, participation partitions, shared entertainment-budget envelopes, barriers, save migration, weekly progression, corruption recovery, and read-only Audience Market evidence are implemented. Current subscribers, revenue, platform allocation, rights, and box office remain owned by existing live systems until WE4 and later phases.

## Purpose

Determine which cohorts are willing and able to participate in each entertainment industry.

## Core work

- calculate separate streaming eligibility, streaming interest, cinema eligibility, cinema interest, and future-industry participation;
- consider connectivity, devices, payment access, theatre availability, affordability, leisure time, culture, age, and content awareness;
- represent cohorts that participate in both streaming and cinema, only one, or neither;
- reserve cross-industry entertainment budgets without double spending;
- provide stable adapter contracts without implementing the full cinema economy.

## Completion gate

- participation is never assumed from population alone;
- streaming and cinema participation can diverge naturally by country and cohort;
- cross-industry totals and budgets reconcile;
- future adapters can consume the same cohort identities.

# Phase WE4 — Streaming Plan Choice and Market Competition

## Purpose

Make every player, real, fictional, and future streaming platform compete for the same eligible country cohorts.

## Core work

- evaluate exact plan prices, annual and introductory discounts, advertising load, quality, device limits, screens, downloads, bundles, and product features;
- evaluate catalogue depth, recent hits, franchises, genres, local-language availability, subtitle/dub research, platform reputation, reliability, marketing, and loyalty;
- allocate zero, one, or several subscriptions per household within budget and access constraints;
- select actual plans rather than using only a blended legacy ARPU;
- include all eligible AI platforms without a four-platform or other artificial world cap;
- preserve lightweight stats-driven AI strategy.

## Completion gate

- configured plans affect real weekly demand and revenue;
- all platforms compete for one finite audience pool;
- the cheapest and most expensive services can both succeed for explainable reasons;
- country, cohort, plan, and global totals reconcile.

# Phase WE5 — Acquisition, Churn, Sharing, and Piracy

## Purpose

Turn cohort preferences into persistent weekly customer behaviour.

## Core work

- model joins, cancellations, upgrades, downgrades, returns, rotation, and multi-service ownership;
- distinguish paying accounts, paying households, profiles, active viewers, and external shared households;
- model password sharing and piracy as access paths with revenue, reach, infrastructure, reputation, and conversion consequences;
- make enforcement, pricing, releases, marketing, quality, crises, and competitor moves affect behaviour;
- store bounded cohort memory so behaviour has continuity without saving every calculation.

## Completion gate

- subscriber movements can be explained by saved inputs and cohort decisions;
- sharing and piracy never count as direct subscription revenue by mistake;
- churn, acquisition, and reactivation agree across dashboards and finance;
- save/reload does not reroll outcomes.

# Phase WE6 — Viewing, Content Demand, and Revenue Attribution

## Purpose

Translate audience access into title-level attention, engagement, and commercial results.

## Core work

- allocate viewing by genre, language, quality, talent, franchise, marketing, buzz, recency, recommendation technology, accessibility, and competing releases;
- calculate watch hours, starts, completion, repeat viewing, subscriber acquisition, retention value, advertising inventory, premium transactions, rentals, purchases, sponsorship exposure, sharing load, and piracy reach;
- connect canonical rights and territorial availability before allocating a view;
- attribute revenue and demand without paying or counting the same activity twice;
- feed title and catalogue learning back into the existing player and AI decision systems.

## Completion gate

- title, catalogue, platform, country, and global audience totals reconcile;
- a title cannot receive demand where it is unavailable;
- niche, local, prestige, fandom, and mass-market successes emerge from audience fit;
- all revenue streams use canonical activity and configured terms.

# Phase WE7 — Shared Player and AI Platform Economy

## Purpose

Make the cohort world authoritative for both the player's service and all AI-controlled platforms.

## Core work

- connect every existing and generated platform to the same demand allocator;
- let strategy stats select target countries, cohorts, pricing posture, research, localization, content, marketing, sharing policy, expansion, and withdrawal;
- preserve existing AI cost bias and assistance only while AI-controlled;
- ensure acquired platforms expose coherent subscribers, plans, countries, cohort mix, catalogue demand, sharing exposure, finances, and history;
- remove or demote parallel decorative audience estimates once canonical projections replace them.

## Completion gate

- player and AI company totals come from the same finite population;
- ownership transfer preserves history and removes AI-only benefits;
- newly generated platforms enter the world through the same interfaces;
- existing Platform AI, rights, content-market, research, localization, and finance flows remain compatible.

# Phase WE8 — Economy, History, Performance, and Save Safety

## Purpose

Make the world simulation balanced, explainable, and sustainable over many generations.

## Core work

- use sparse cohort state, deterministic regeneration, bounded ledgers, periodic snapshots, and safe history compression;
- add schema versioning, migrations, integrity checks, recovery, and malformed-save handling;
- test recessions, inflation, booms, ageing, connectivity growth, platform collapse, new entrants, price wars, piracy shocks, and technology changes;
- define mobile week-processing budgets and degradation strategies;
- verify hundreds of game years without population drift, duplicate revenue, runaway subscriptions, save bloat, or progress stoppage;
- provide player-readable explanations and analytics without exposing raw simulation complexity.

## Completion gate

- long-run deterministic and save/reload tests pass;
- week progression remains within the agreed mobile performance budget;
- saved history remains bounded without deleting material legacy records;
- financial, audience, and population totals remain reconcilable;
- casual play requires no cohort micromanagement.

# Phase WE9 — Cinema and Box Office Adapter

## Purpose

Connect the future cinema-chain and box-office systems to the same population rather than generating a second audience universe.

## Core work

- consume country cohorts, entertainment budgets, awareness, taste, access, and economic state;
- calculate unique moviegoers separately from tickets and repeat footfalls;
- consider screen availability, distance, ticket and concession prices, premium formats, social habits, reviews, marketing, stars, franchises, piracy, streaming substitution, and release windows;
- allocate country-level attendance and revenue without requiring universal cinema participation;
- allow cinema chains to compete for accessible local footfall;
- return cinema outcomes to fame, cultural reach, future demand, studio finance, and media systems.

## Timing

WE9 defines the shared contract now but its full implementation remains part of the future cinema-chain and box-office project.

## Completion gate

- every cinema footfall originates from an eligible cohort;
- unique viewers, admissions, repeat visits, tickets, and revenue remain distinct;
- streaming and cinema can complement or substitute for one another;
- country box-office totals reconcile with theatre and distributor records.

## Player-facing philosophy

The underlying system may be deep, but the ordinary player sees concise consequences instead of cohort administration. Examples include:

- a country gained value-plan households;
- premium pricing works among high-income fandom audiences;
- sharing increased reach but weakened paid conversion;
- insufficient local-language coverage raised churn;
- a hit attracted viewers but did not retain them after release;
- weak affordability reduced cinema admissions despite strong awareness.

Advanced reports may reveal country, cohort, plan, participation, and title breakdowns. Casual players can continue operating through pricing, catalogue, research, marketing, and strategy choices already present in the game.

## Implementation sequence

1. Implement and verify WE1.
2. Implement WE2 on top of WE1.
3. Implement WE3 and freeze the first industry-adapter interface.
4. Connect streaming through WE4–WE7.
5. Complete long-run balance and save hardening in WE8.
6. Preserve the WE9 interface until the cinema project begins, then implement it against the same canonical world.

Each phase requires its own approved specification, implementation plan, focused audits, production build, save migration coverage where applicable, and a completion report before the next phase begins.

## Programme exit condition

The programme is complete when population and cohorts are canonical; every streaming platform draws demand from that finite world; actual configured plans and content influence real weekly behaviour and revenue; sharing and piracy are distinct from paying subscriptions; long-running saves remain safe and performant; and the future cinema system can consume the same people without inventing another audience population.
