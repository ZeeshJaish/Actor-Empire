## The playable model

The streaming world has one canonical identity for each real project and one rights authority. A platform's catalogue contains contracted rights, not copies of films. The market stays lively from three sources: the existing 2,000-title historical library (including marquee titles), real AI/player studio productions and commissions, and generated market packages. A three-week cycle produces **25 player-visible opportunity cards**: a proposed opening mix of **20 individual, complete but dormant films/series** plus **5 collections of existing, rights-valid titles**. A collection is one card, not five extra newly generated projects. Mix and prices must be balance-tested rather than treated as guaranteed hit counts.

Generated individual packages have title, logline, language, genre, format, producer/seller, writer and script credit, director, cast, realistic deal terms, forecast quality, and optional talent participation **at offer creation**. They are not empty placeholders. Their named people and creative details remain saved on the offer. Until acquisition, however, they do not enter the canonical project list, NPC career/wealth, studio accounts, news, awards, reviews, or Upcoming. Unsold offers expire at the end of their window. A successful purchase promotes exactly one canonical project, settles the rights purchase and seller receipt, activates the relevant NPC/studio credit and contract, and can create restrained deal buzz. Reviews, viewership, release results, and award eligibility wait for the actual public premiere. A premium/featured package is more expensive and has high potential, never a guaranteed award or audience result.

The existing **Upcoming / hot projects** surface is reserved for genuine studio productions or commissions that can be in development, filming, or awaiting delivery. Those remain in-world even if no streamer buys. Generated market packages never enter Upcoming, including premium ones. Trade news can tease a genuine studio project before release; an unsold generated offer must vanish without world-news residue.

## Decisions and trade-offs

1. **Recommended: one world, two discovery lanes.** The player sees a shared public supply with protected early first-look entries. Rival AI competes for appropriate public listings and also sources from its own originals, the historical rights-valid library, genuine studio Upcoming sales, and a brokered supply lane. Brokered acquisitions become ordinary canonical world projects/contracts with unique IDs. This prevents 33 wealthy rivals from clearing the player's shelf while keeping one coherent universe. AI acquisition is bounded by treasury runway, catalogue need, strategy, per-cycle capacity, rights compatibility, and diminishing returns.
2. One public pool with unrestricted wealthy-rival purchasing is simpler but likely makes the player market empty. Waiting to let rivals buy only player leftovers is too biased and weakens believable competition.
3. A wholly separate permanent player-only universe would guarantee stock but duplicate project identities, rights and award histories. It is rejected. **Paid scouting** may create a temporary player-first discovery batch, but anything acquired is promoted into the same world.

The initial balance target is 25 player-visible cards every three weeks, not 25 mandatory rival purchases. Collections must use existing valid titles and respect each component's territory, duration, exclusivity and price. Rival brokered supply is not another uncontrolled 20 full new films every cycle; prefer existing titles and generate only the additional packages needed to meet bounded catalogue targets. No platform can purchase without funds or a compatible right.

## Talent contracts for first films, sequels, and AI studios

For **every** player Greenlight, including the first film, an eligible actor, director or credited writer may be offered an optional backend share. Default is **0 points**. The player explicitly moves it in one-percentage-point steps; the initial suggested UI range is **0–3 points per person and 10 points total per project**, both shown in the contract UI. This is a **proposed balance choice for review**, not an assertion about existing behavior. Upfront salary remains separate. Returning/sequel talent may make higher cash and/or backend demands; the player may accept, counter, choose a different person, or decline where existing negotiation rules permit. No share is implied merely because a project is a sequel.

The contract stores stable talent ID, role, upfront fee, backend percentage, entitlement basis, and a settlement key. Backend is a percentage of the **studio's actually collected, title-attributed receipts** after external distributor/platform rights-holder allocations, not a percentage of the platform's subscriber revenue, gross box office, or the whole studio's cash. Only positive eligible receipts pay. One receipt event can pay each participant once; reversals/returns are handled by explicit adjustment entries, not rerunning a formula. The studio records the expense, the paid NPC receives personal income/net-worth impact, and credit/awards resolve through the stable NPC ID. Producer-studio wealth is not silently credited to its owner personally. Existing completed contracts default to zero and must not be retroactively paid.

AI studios use the same payment/credit ledger, with deterministic talent selection, believable upfront offers, and bounded optional backend for roles whose leverage merits it. Their project budgets must include commitments, their milestone spending and eventual receipts must not double charge talent, and a failed/held production does not generate imaginary profit. This extends existing AI studio production rather than fabricating an unrelated Greenlight screen.

## Writer identities and awards

Add a curated library of **original fictional** writers with stable IDs, language/genre strengths, fees, career and net-worth fields; preserve existing player scripts and legacy writer strings. Avoid one-letter celebrity parodies. A real saved writer link becomes an NPC credit only when hired/contracted for an actual project. Generated offer packages record a complete, already-made film and its stable writer, cast and director without new bookings, payment, Forbes movement or résumé change while dormant. Promotion at acquisition performs the actual credit/contract activation exactly once; it does not re-book a completed shoot or silently change advertised names. Fixed talent cash shares for a generated package come out of its saved producer/seller settlement, with a nonnegative seller floor, rather than creating a second buyer charge or pretending past production expenses occurred. Any participation points apply only to future eligible receipts.

AI studio commitments and canonical released `IndustryProject` records gain optional writer ID/name fields (legacy records remain valid). Screenplay nominations and wins resolve the credited writer, not the lead actor. Awards stay tied to the actual release year and eligibility rules. Existing awards are immutable; absent legacy writer identity gets a truthful studio/team or unknown-writer fallback, not an invented celebrity. NPC credits, income and awards affect career/Forbes only after the appropriate acquisition, receipt or ceremony event.

## Paid refresh and screen behavior

Both paid refresh actions start at **$450,000**. Development Lab pays from the production studio treasury; Content Market scouting pays from the owned streaming platform treasury. UI labels, affordability checks and debit logic use one shared pricing rule rather than stale hardcoded $250,000 text. Insufficient funds show the shortfall and do not charge. Reopening a screen never rerolls or charges.

**Approved 2026-09-27 correction:** paid scouting is unlimited while funded, with a separate counter per production studio and owned streaming platform. Within each fixed three-game-week cycle, the next price is **$450,000 × (successful paid scouts this cycle + 1)**: $450K, $900K, $1.35M, and $45M for the 100th. Each counter resets on the next cycle. This is linear escalation, not literal exponential doubling. A paid refresh changes that business's current discovery shelf, not the canonical global market cycle or another platform's listings. The next scheduled free cycle still occurs on its normal absolute-week boundary; paying does not postpone it. Streaming paid scouting provides up to 25 new player-first **cards**, mixing complete dormant packages and valid existing-title collections, while the public shelf remains accessible. If distinct, rights-valid supply cannot produce a materially new shelf, the action fails without a charge or counter increment. Old unsigned paid-scout cards may be withdrawn; signed deals, active negotiations/auction obligations, first-look protections and canonical studio Upcoming sales survive. Scout-batch generation and payment commit together, with stable IDs keyed by cycle and ordinal; failure leaves money and shelf unchanged. No free retries or double charges after interrupted save/import.

The new action is placed within each screen's existing visual system, typography, spacing, button hierarchy, money notation and mobile navigation. The streaming Content Market's current gate/market/detail/collections/Upcoming distinctions stay legible; Development Lab keeps its own production-house look. Show the next escalating price, source treasury, and expected scope before confirmation. During a refresh, disable repeat taps, retain the prior shelf until success, and show a modest “Scouting new scripts…” working state **only if the work lasts roughly one second**; display real progress when measurable and clear it as soon as completion/failure occurs. Do not invent a countdown or delay a fast operation. Provide accessible status text and safe-area/phone-height coverage.

## Save, timing, and world invariants

- Generation runs at the weekly world/cycle boundary with a versioned stable seed and absolute week. UI opening only reads saved offers. The paid-scout action is an explicit deterministic saved transition, not `Math.random()` on component mount. No title duplication across public, private scouting and rival brokered lanes.
- One transaction promotes a purchased generated package into a unique canonical ID, registers compatible rights, debits buyer, credits seller, records participant contracts and processed keys. A licence is not underlying IP ownership. Public premiere remains a separate event.
- Existing 2,000-title library and 16 marquee titles are not regenerated or lost; old permanent rights retain their meaning. Migrate new state lazily on old saves, without invented back-history or replayed charges. Preserve released project references required by awards, royalties and rights through compaction/export/import.
- Bound offer and contract retention, rival decisions and save size for decades of play and 33 platforms. Record all monetary events once; replaying a week must be observationally identical.
- Featured offers may get market-surface promotion. World news before acquisition is limited to **genuine** studio productions. Acquisition and eventual premiere can each create correctly timed, distinct news facts.

## Explicit boundaries and review choices

No real film/IMDb database, copied real-person writer identities, guaranteed hit/award, automatic player audience grant, retrospective payments for existing projects, or automatic rights renewal. The first balance version uses 20+5 cards, 0–3 points per participant and 10 total points **subject to review**; this is not a promise that every batch contains a prestige success. The paid-scout frequency and linear price rule were approved on 2026-09-27; phase 8 balance and long-save closure remains separate.
