# Platform AI Phase 4: Research, Markets, and Localization

## Status

Approved in chat on 2026-08-27. This document locks the Phase 4 design before implementation. It does not authorize staging or committing unrelated or Phase 4 files.

## Purpose

Phase 4 makes AI-controlled streaming companies develop technology, enter countries, and localize individual titles through persistent investment rather than automatic capability grants.

It connects the earlier phases:

```text
Phase 2 content plan
→ Phase 3 canonical delivered production or rights-ready title
→ Phase 4 active-country, technology, language, and localization readiness
→ Phase 6 scheduling and streaming release
```

The player-owned streaming company and AI rivals use the same canonical research, technology, market, language, and localization definitions. AI-controlled companies receive only bounded, explicit operating efficiencies. Those efficiencies stop after player acquisition.

## Scope

Phase 4 includes:

- Mature starting profiles for the five existing AI platforms.
- A future-compatible operating profile for regional and fictional platforms.
- Dynamic one-to-four concurrent research capacity.
- Portfolio-level affordability and runway checks.
- Persistent research, IP, installation, and operating stages.
- Bounded AI-only cost and lead-time efficiencies.
- Country-level expansion decisions and persistent market operations.
- A shared localization research tree.
- Separate subtitle and dubbing levels.
- Exact supported languages.
- Per-title, per-language localization jobs.
- Country-specific comprehension, reach, and appreciation consequences.
- Phase 2 offer and Phase 3 delivery integration.
- Acquisition handoff to player-standard rules.
- Deterministic migration, exact-once payments, diagnostics, and focused audits.

Phase 4 does not include:

- Adding all future regional or fictional companies to the live world.
- Redesigning rival cards or Platform Wars presentation.
- The shared News and X reaction-template engine.
- Streaming scheduling, performance settlement, awards, or platform learning.
- The full economy/distress replacement belonging to Phase 5.

## Canonical architecture

One shared domain supplies research definitions, technology installations, country requirements, language definitions, localization quotes, and result modifiers.

Controllers sit above that shared domain:

- The player controller presents choices and charges player-standard terms.
- The AI controller ranks deterministic choices, applies an allowed efficiency profile, and records the reason.
- The ownership resolver decides which controller may act.

There must not be a simplified parallel AI research or localization engine. A capability installed by AI must remain a valid capability if the player later acquires the company.

## Platform operating profiles

Each platform has a declarative operating profile containing:

- Company scale: `REGIONAL`, `GROWTH`, `MATURE`, or `GLOBAL`.
- Home-country IDs.
- Starting active-country IDs.
- Historically installed research and technology definition IDs.
- Starting subtitle and dubbing operating tiers.
- Starting supported languages.
- Research-organization capacity.
- Expansion and localization ambition.
- AI operating-efficiency policy.
- Existing strategic, creative, commercial, financial, and technology competence.

### Existing companies

Netflix, Apple TV+, Disney+, Hulu, and YouTube begin as established services, not newly founded startups.

Their starting state is curated from game design:

- Netflix begins with broad international markets and strong delivery, recommendations, and localization operations.
- Apple TV+ begins with strong playback, reliability, security, and technology operations.
- Disney+ begins with strong global family/franchise distribution and localization operations.
- Hulu begins with a more concentrated footprint and lower global operating breadth.
- YouTube begins with exceptionally broad delivery reach and strong advertising, playback, and platform technology.

The exact live country list remains drawn from the canonical country-market catalogue. At minimum, Netflix's starting profile includes the United States, India, and Japan.

Starting mature capabilities are represented as deterministic historical `OPERATING` research/installations, not unexplained numeric levels. They do not retroactively charge the company's current cash. Frontier technology remains unresearched so mature companies still have progression.

### Save migration

New saves receive the complete starting profile.

Legacy saves follow preserve-first migration:

- Existing saved active markets win.
- Existing saved research and installed technology win.
- Missing profile-backed historical assets are added only where the old save has no canonical value.
- Migration never downgrades or rerolls progress.
- Re-running normalization produces byte-equivalent canonical state.

### Future platforms

The profile format must support future regional and fictional services without introducing a new simulation path.

Examples include an India-only service, Korean-drama specialist, European prestige service, ad-funded regional platform, or well-funded global entrant. A regional company may start with one home country and already own selected technology or language capability. Later expansion uses the same Phase 4 system as every other platform.

Actual creation, branding, and UI placement of those companies is deferred.

## Dynamic research capacity

The fixed two-programme limit is removed. A platform may have one to four active research programmes.

### Base capacity

- `REGIONAL`: one.
- `GROWTH`: two.
- `MATURE`: two.
- `GLOBAL`: three.

A healthy platform can earn one additional slot, up to the hard maximum of four, when:

- It is not distressed or under a research freeze.
- Its technology organization can manage the extra programme.
- All currently committed production, rights, localization, market, and research obligations remain funded.
- Post-commit cash stays above its required reserve target plus obligations due inside the planning horizon.
- The new programme has positive strategic value after cash strain and overlap penalties.

The fourth slot is therefore normally available only to healthy, well-run global or exceptionally strong mature companies. Excess cash alone is insufficient when the platform lacks operational capacity.

### Portfolio affordability

Affordability is evaluated across the full active portfolio. The AI cannot approve four programmes separately using the same unreserved cash.

The projection includes:

- Immediate research cost.
- Expected IP cost.
- Expected installation cost.
- Recurring research and installed-technology operating cost.
- Held one-time obligations.
- Committed production and rights funding.
- Active market expansion.
- Planned localization.
- The platform's target runway.

Committed future stages are represented in the decision forecast even when the cash is not yet charged. Actual payment remains at the canonical stage boundary and occurs exactly once.

### Research choice

Every eligible programme receives a deterministic priority derived from:

- Capability gap.
- Active market blockers.
- Upcoming slate requirements.
- Language and localization gaps.
- Content-operation congestion.
- Rival capability pressure.
- Platform strategy and personality.
- Expected audience, revenue, or operating benefit.
- Immediate and lifecycle cost.
- Runway after commitment.
- Existing related or overlapping work.

The AI chooses a compatible portfolio, not merely the individually highest raw scores. Replaying the same save, platform, planning cycle, and candidates produces the same result.

### Research lifecycle

```text
RESEARCHING
→ PROTOTYPING
→ TESTING
→ AWAITING_IP
→ READY_TO_INSTALL
→ INSTALLING
→ OPERATING
```

Research completion unlocks installation eligibility only. The target technology level becomes active only when installation reaches `OPERATING`.

Distressed platforms may progress work already under contract but cannot start discretionary programmes while research is frozen.

## Bounded AI operating efficiency

AI competitors need a modest scale and coordination advantage to remain competitive with an attentive player. The advantage is explicit profile data, not hidden cash or free capability.

An AI efficiency policy contains the applicable subset of these explicit fields:

- Research cost multiplier.
- Installation cost multiplier.
- Market-entry cost multiplier.
- Localization cost multiplier.
- Administrative lead-time multiplier.
- Localization-throughput multiplier.
- Access to a local-partnership market-entry route.

Normal tuning bands are:

- Five to twenty percent cost efficiency.
- Up to approximately fifteen percent administrative or coordination-time efficiency.
- No automatic creative-quality bonus.

Safeguards:

- Essential security, reliability, rights, and language requirements cannot be bypassed.
- A local partnership can satisfy a soft operational gap only through a recorded cost, recurring obligation, and any associated performance limitation.
- AI efficiency never grants cash.
- AI efficiency never creates an unsupported language or localization asset.
- The multiplier and its applied saving are frozen into the committed quote and remain auditable while the company stays AI-controlled. Acquisition preserves amounts already paid but removes the modifier from remaining time and unpaid stages.
- Platform competence improves choice quality and efficiency within these bounds; it does not guarantee outcomes.

## Country market expansion

Country access and title comprehension are separate systems.

A platform can operate in a country while individual foreign-language titles still perform poorly there.

### Expansion choice

Country priority considers:

- Addressable streaming audience.
- Growth rate.
- Competition.
- Entry and recurring costs.
- Rights and regulatory requirements.
- Delivery, security, reliability, and content-operation requirements.
- Languages used by the country.
- Existing language capability.
- Catalogue and upcoming-slate fit.
- Platform strategy.
- Cash runway and concurrent expansion load.

A country already active or already in a non-terminal expansion operation cannot be selected again.

### Expansion lifecycle

The canonical market-operation lifecycle remains persistent. It may include planned, awaiting-funding, clearance, suspended, ready, active, and exited states as supported by the shared market core.

Entry costs are charged exactly once. Additional clearance requirements remain explicit. Active countries are derived from canonical active market operations rather than a separate magical reach number.

An India-first future platform begins with India active. Entering Japan, the United Kingdom, or the United States requires a real expansion operation.

## Shared localization technology

Localization uses the same research and installed-capability model for the player and AI.

The progression contains:

```text
Localization Foundation
├─ Subtitling Operations L1 → L2 → L3
└─ Dubbing Operations L1 → L2 → L3
   └─ Advanced simultaneous localization
```

Exact technology names and installation targets reuse or extend the canonical player streaming research catalogue.

### Tier effects

Higher subtitle or dubbing tiers improve bounded operational factors such as:

- Maximum simultaneous jobs.
- Lead time.
- Unit cost.
- Translation and adaptation quality.
- Dubbing quality.
- Multi-country synchronization.
- Resistance to cultural-adaptation mistakes.

Research never localizes a title by itself. Every title still requires an explicit paid job unless its original language already satisfies the target audience.

## Language capability

Global subtitle and dubbing tiers are not enough. Each platform stores exact language capability.

For every supported language, persist:

- Language ID.
- Subtitle level from zero to three.
- Dubbing level from zero to three.
- Capability source or historical profile provenance.
- Activation/completion identity where applicable.

Language expansion uses regional operating packages backed by exact language IDs. Packages avoid dozens of repetitive research programmes, while the resulting capability remains precise. The AI chooses packages using active countries, planned countries, current slate languages, and future content needs.

A platform's home and historically supported languages can begin active. New language support requires the appropriate foundation, operating tier, package, and cost.

## Per-title localization jobs

A localization job is canonical to:

- One platform.
- One content plan.
- One canonical project.
- One target language.
- One mode: subtitle or dub.
- One or more covered active countries that require that language.
- One immutable quote and efficiency snapshot.

The job stores disclosed cost, lead time, quality forecast, capability tier at commitment, obligation ID, creation week, start week, ready week, and terminal status.

Its lifecycle is:

```text
WAITING_FOR_FUNDS → IN_PROGRESS → READY
```

`CANCELLED` remains available for invalidated or terminal plans.

Rules:

- Jobs can be planned only for a canonical project belonging to the content plan.
- The requested language and mode must be supported at commitment.
- Every covered country must already have one canonical `ACTIVE` market operation. Localization for a planned expansion begins only after that market activates.
- Funding settlement starts the job exactly once.
- Insufficient cash leaves it waiting without inventing debt or readiness in Phase 4.
- Save normalization removes forged, orphaned, duplicated, or terminal unpaid jobs without replaying payments.

## Localization promises and release freedom

The player-facing promise remains:

- None.
- Subtitles.
- Dubs and subtitles.

That summary resolves into exact required languages and jobs underneath.

A localization promise becomes a hard readiness requirement when it is part of:

- An accepted rights agreement.
- A commission or distribution contract.
- A selected release plan.
- A mandatory country requirement.

Otherwise, a platform may release a foreign-language title without localization and accept the consequences. Phase 4 records readiness and consequence inputs; Phase 6 applies them to the streaming result.

A platform cannot promise localization that its currently operating research, tier, language support, active markets, and available capacity cannot deliver. Planned future research is not sufficient to promise it now.

## Country-language performance model

Every title has one or more original languages. Every country has canonical audience-language preferences.

For each title-country pair, derive a comprehension state:

- `NATIVE_OR_COMPATIBLE`.
- `DUBBED`.
- `SUBTITLED`.
- `UNLOCALIZED`.

The state contributes to later reach, completion, appreciation, churn, and reputation calculations.

Ordering rules:

- Native or compatible audio gives full comprehension.
- High-quality dubbing gives the strongest mass-market accessibility for a foreign-language title.
- Subtitles materially improve comprehension but have audience- and genre-dependent reach.
- No localization leaves only niche, multilingual, diaspora, or unusually motivated demand.
- Poor dubbing can expand reach while reducing appreciation.
- Strong content can partially overcome a language mismatch but cannot make comprehension irrelevant.
- Family and mass-market content benefit more from dubbing; prestige and younger international audiences may accept subtitles more strongly.

Example: a Korean-language title may launch in India without Hindi or English localization, but it receives sharply reduced mainstream reach. Subtitles improve it substantially. A high-quality Hindi dub provides the strongest mass accessibility, while final performance still depends on title quality, marketing, discovery, and audience fit.

Phase 4 supplies deterministic inputs and tunable disclosed modifiers. Phase 6 owns final performance settlement.

## Phase 2 and Phase 3 integration

### Phase 2 offers

Localization terms in bids and rights offers must be capability-backed.

The platform may offer `NONE`, `SUBTITLES`, or `DUBS_AND_SUBTITLES` only when the exact intended country and language scope is supportable. Accepting a promise creates or reserves the downstream localization requirement; it does not create a free ready asset.

### Phase 3 delivery

After an original is canonically delivered:

1. Resolve intended active release countries.
2. Read the project's original language.
3. Resolve required country languages.
4. Determine promised and strategically selected localization.
5. Create supported per-language jobs.
6. Wait for funding and lead time.
7. Mark localization readiness without releasing the title.

Delivery still does not create a theatrical or streaming release.

## Player acquisition handoff

When `resolvePlatformController` changes to `PLAYER`:

- No new AI research, market, language, or localization decision is allowed.
- AI cost and time efficiencies stop for all new commitments.
- Completed research, installed technology, supported languages, and active countries remain assets.
- Paid progress is preserved.
- Already-settled localization contracts retain their frozen terms.
- Unpaid future research/IP/installation stages use player-standard pricing.
- Remaining research or installation time is recalculated at player-standard speed without restarting completed work.
- Additional market-clearance costs arising after acquisition use player-standard terms.
- Player interfaces become the only authority for future choices.

No previously paid amount is retroactively increased, and no installed asset is deleted.

## Persistence and determinism

All Phase 4 decisions use stable IDs and deterministic seeded choices. `Math.random()` and `Date.now()` are forbidden in Platform AI Phase 4 logic.

Persist or deterministically derive:

- Operating-profile provenance.
- Installed technology and historical operating research.
- Active research portfolio.
- Research capacity and its decision inputs.
- Market operations and active countries.
- Subtitle and dubbing tiers.
- Exact language capabilities.
- Per-title localization jobs.
- Frozen AI-efficiency snapshots.
- One-time obligations and settlements.
- Decision explanations.
- Acquisition handoff state.

Replaying or reloading the same absolute week must not create another charge, job, market operation, research programme, language unlock, or decision.

## Observability

Normal rival-card and Platform Wars redesign is deferred at the user's direction.

Phase 4 stores all data required for future presentation:

- Active and completed research.
- Technology strengths.
- Active and expanding countries.
- Subtitle and dubbing tiers.
- Supported languages.
- Waiting, active, and ready localization jobs.
- Capability-blocked plans.
- Decision reasons and applied AI efficiencies.

A QA/cheat action may expose a compact technical snapshot and deterministic progression controls for testing. It must not become a second simulation route.

## Failure handling

- No affordable research portfolio: start nothing and record no charge.
- Research/IP/installation funding unavailable: remain at the current unpaid stage.
- Country entry funding unavailable: remain waiting or reject commitment without partial duplicate charges.
- Mandatory language unsupported: keep the plan blocked and do not forge a job.
- Optional localization unsupported: allow a later unlocalized release path with consequences.
- Localization funding unavailable: remain `WAITING_FOR_FUNDS`.
- Acquired platform: return unchanged from every AI-only action.
- Invalid legacy data: normalize to canonical definitions while preserving valid paid progress.

## Testing strategy

Implementation follows focused RED-to-GREEN slices.

Required audits cover:

1. Mature starting profiles create canonical historical operating assets without retroactive cash changes.
2. Legacy saves preserve valid markets, research, technology, languages, and paid work.
3. Dynamic research capacity ranges from one to four and respects scale, operations, runway, and distress.
4. Four separate candidates cannot reuse the same unreserved cash.
5. Research choice and portfolio choice are deterministic.
6. Research, IP, and installation charge once at the correct boundaries.
7. Capability remains locked until installation is operating.
8. AI efficiency stays inside configured bounds and records the applied saving.
9. Hard requirements cannot be bypassed by efficiency.
10. Country expansion uses canonical markets, costs, requirements, and active operations.
11. Regional future-platform fixtures start locally and expand through the normal system.
12. Subtitle and dubbing tiers remain independent.
13. Exact language support gates localization jobs.
14. Native, dubbed, subtitled, and unlocalized states produce correctly ordered readiness/performance inputs.
15. Mandatory promises block readiness; optional missing localization supplies a consequence instead.
16. Localization waits for funds, progresses by disclosed lead time, and settles once.
17. Forged, duplicate, orphaned, or terminal unpaid jobs cannot survive normalization.
18. Acquisition stops AI decisions and efficiencies while preserving completed assets and paid progress.
19. Same-week and save/reload replay remain idempotent.
20. Existing Platform AI sourcing, production, bidding, economy, release, turn, acquisition, and save audits remain green.

The stale research audit assertion must use the canonical runtime schema version rather than hard-coding version 6. After that correction, the complete research/market audit must run to completion; the current first failure does not prove that later assertions pass.

## Completion criteria

Phase 4 is complete when:

- Research capacity is dynamically one to four.
- Portfolio affordability prevents duplicated runway assumptions.
- Existing companies begin with appropriate mature assets.
- Future regional and fictional operating profiles are supported without a parallel engine.
- AI efficiencies are bounded, recorded, and removed after acquisition.
- Completed assets and paid progress survive acquisition.
- Country access and per-title comprehension remain separate.
- Subtitle and dubbing tiers are independent.
- Exact languages are persisted and enforced.
- Foreign-language content can proceed without optional localization but receives deterministic consequence inputs.
- Contractually promised localization remains a hard readiness requirement.
- All research, market, language, and localization payments occur exactly once.
- Phase 4 state survives save/reload deterministically.
- Player-controlled platforms receive no AI mutation or automatic decision.
- Focused Phase 4 audits, relevant regressions, build, and source-integrity checks pass or any unrelated pre-existing failures are explicitly reported.
- No rival presentation redesign or News/X reaction engine is silently added.

## Master-plan amendment

The original Phase 4 wording is refined as follows:

- “Limited simultaneous research” means a dynamically affordable one-to-four portfolio, not a fixed limit of two.
- Existing global platforms receive mature historical starting assets.
- Future regional and fictional platforms use declarative starting profiles and the same lifecycle.
- AI-controlled companies receive bounded operating efficiencies that stop after acquisition.
- Localization remains summarized as none, subtitles, or dubs and subtitles in offers, but is implemented through independent tiers, exact languages, and per-title jobs.
- Active country access does not guarantee that foreign-language content performs well there.
- Rival presentation remains deferred while canonical data is stored now.
